For a research prototype, the tradeoff is usually between ambition and realism:
5% error target: Very strong scientific performance. Predictions would likely be considered highly trustworthy, but this may be difficult to achieve given biological variability and the limited number of genes currently modeled.
10% error target: Often a reasonable research goal. Predictions are accurate enough to support biological hypotheses while still being achievable for an early-stage model.
20% error target: More appropriate for a proof-of-concept. It can demonstrate that the model captures general trends, but users may be less confident using it to draw conclusions about specific interventions.
For example, if the observed median lifespan is 20 days:
5% error = ±1 day
10% error = ±2 days
20% error = ±4 days
