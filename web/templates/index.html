How does this work?
  1. Researchers measured how active each gene was in five kinds of worms: normal worms and four strains with changed genes.
  2. Those measurements go into a computer model of the worm's metabolism (the chemical reactions that keep it alive), which works out how fast the worm can grow.
  3. From the model's output, the site estimates how quickly the worm ages and how long a typical worm of that kind lives.
What do the results mean?
  • Median lifespan is the age at which half the worms have died. It's in the model's time units, not days.
  • Mean lifespan is the average age at death across all the worms.
  • Growth compared with a normal worm: 1.0000 means the same as a normal worm, and lower means slower.

These numbers come from a research model, not lab measurements. The model only covers the five worm types in the dataset, so other gene combinations can't be predicted yet.
